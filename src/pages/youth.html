What your child builds here

The kicks and blocks are the fun part. What parents notice at home and at school is everything that comes with them.

  • Focus

    Classes train concentration the same way they train a stance: a little longer and a little steadier each week.

  • Self-discipline and respect

    Students bow in, listen, and learn that effort is what earns the next stripe.

  • Better at other sports

    Balance, coordination and flexibility carry over to everything else they play.

  • “I can do it”

    Setting a goal and reaching it, again and again, is how confidence gets built.

Four stripes, then a new belt

Students learn to set short-term and long-term goals by earning stripes on their belts. Think of each stripe as a quiz, and the belt test as the exam it prepares them for.

Through the work of earning stripes and belts, students learn to never give up. Our part is to guide them with a loving yet disciplined hand.

  1. 1

    Memory

    The language and culture of karate. For white belts, that means counting from one to ten in Japanese.

  2. 2

    Bronze

    A foundational stance and a defensive block. Karate is for protecting and perfecting yourself, and this stripe stands for that.

  3. 3

    Silver

    Control of basic strikes and kicks, and the responsibility of knowing when it is right, and wrong, to use them.

  4. 4

    Gold

    The kata taught that session: a traditional pattern of techniques handed down through generations of Okinawan karate.

Belt tests for ages 7 and up are held three times a year, at the end of January, May and September. More about stripes and belt tests

From the mat to the classroom

It’s a story we’ve heard time and again: a student arrives struggling at school, and within months the focus they practice here shows up in their grades.

It’s also why many of our students go on to qualify for the American Shudokan Association’s college scholarship. Every actively training black belt who finishes high school with at least a B average receives $1,000 toward their education.

Karate changed our founder’s life too. Tobey Stansbury started at seven with a severe stutter. Within months of training it was gone, and today he lectures to large groups and teaches seminars around the world.
