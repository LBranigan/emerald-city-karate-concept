What is karate?

Karate is more than an effective means of self-defense or a dynamic sport. It is a vehicle to improve one’s life by refining one’s character through our training on the mat.

The word “karate” is a Japanese word that literally means “empty hand.” The most basic understanding of this word is that you will learn to protect yourself and your loved ones without the need for anything but your own hands. Through karate training you will learn that the true meaning of karate is much deeper than a simple literal translation. As your understanding improves you will be able to face the challenges that life throws at you with confidence, determination, and peace of mind.

Emerald City Karate traces its lineage hundreds of years, all the way back to China and the original importers of martial arts to the Okinawan Islands. This proud heritage has been carried on by martial arts pioneers in the United States like Yokou Takahashi, Walter Todd, and Morris Mack. We are proud to welcome you to join these time-honored traditions in our school. Join us today and start your journey towards a black belt.

From teacher to student

The line that reaches our mat, oldest first, as it is drawn on the Shudokan lineage chart.

  1. Itosu AnkoThe Okinawan master who was Toyama Kanken’s primary teacher. Toyama became his assistant in Shuri in 1907.
  2. Toyama Kanken 1888 to 1966Opened the Shudokan dojo in Tokyo in 1930. Read about Toyama Kanken
  3. Takahashi YokouA direct disciple of Toyama, and Walter Todd’s teacher in the United States.
  4. Walter Todd 1927 to 1999Appointed by Toyama as branch director for the United States. Read about Walter Todd
  5. Morris MackHanshi Morris Mack, one of the Northwest’s pioneering martial arts masters, and Tobey Stansbury’s teacher.
  6. Tobey StansburyFounder of Emerald City Karate, training in Shudokan karate-do since 1988. Meet the instructors
