What thirty minutes on the mat does

Every class is exciting enough that children can’t wait to come back. Underneath the fun are the building blocks for doing well inside the dojo and outside it.

  • Listening and focus

    Our youngest students get better at paying attention and following directions, which is what success at school is built on.

  • An “I can do it” attitude

    A positive self-image becomes a habit, one small challenge passed at a time.

  • An athletic head start

    Motor skills develop quickly at this age, and what they practice here gives them an edge in other sports.

A life skill in every class

Each class works on one life skill, woven into the karate so smoothly that students often don’t notice they’re picking it up. They just know they’re having a blast.

Every skill a child shows earns a stripe on their belt. When all of their life-skill stripes are in place, they’re ready to move up to the next Little Dragons belt. Every Little Dragons belt is white, with colored stripes running along it.

Earning stripes, then belts, teaches children to set short-term and long-term goals and reach them, which is good practice for school.

  • Focus

    Better listening and quicker reactions. Children pick up any physical activity faster and become more attentive students at school.

  • Teamwork

    The more comfortable a child is working with others, the more they accomplish. Team players make friends easily and grow into leaders.

  • Balance and coordination

    Important to develop early. Children who feel confident in what their bodies can do join in more with sports and activities.

  • Memory

    Exercise for the brain. The sooner a child exercises their memory, the stronger it gets, and our drills challenge it constantly.

  • Discipline and control

    Our instructors use drills that show how rewarding discipline can be. Children take pride in doing the right thing and making good decisions.

  • Stranger safety

    Children learn how to “play it safe,” taught in a way that never frightens them. Fire, home and traffic safety come up through the year too.

  • Confidence

    We reinforce that every child is “special, smart and strong,” with drills designed to build belief in their own abilities.

  • Respect

    Being respectful means showing honor. The instructors set the example by being respectful to the students.

  • Compassion

    Showing kindness to those in need. Drills and activities help children see the difference it makes in another person’s life.

Parents are welcome on the mat

If your child feels shy or hesitant, come and join in. Stay for the whole class, or step in to hold pads and cheer during drills. Parents train free in Little Dragons, and being involved is one of the best things you can do for your child.
