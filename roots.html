About Emerald City Karate, Seattle

Roots Dojo was born from the spirit of Emerald City Karate in Seattle, Washington, a dojo known for its warm, family-friendly atmosphere and deep respect for Okinawan tradition.

Roots Dojo continues that legacy here in Japan, blending classical karate, practical self-defense, and bilingual instruction in a community where everyone is welcome.

About Roots Dojo

At Roots Dojo, we train hard, smile often, and grow together. Every class includes:

  • Okinawan karate fundamentals
  • Modern, practical self-defense
  • A fun game or drill to keep energy high
  • Simple Japanese and English phrases woven naturally into training
  • Manners, respect, and dojo etiquette
  • Global and Japanese proverbs that spark reflection and character growth

Our goal is to help each student, child or adult, build confidence, focus, strength, and community. The dojo is a joyful, uplifting space where beginners feel welcome and advanced students feel challenged.

Programs and classes

Youth karate, ages 7 to 12

Active, encouraging classes where kids learn strong karate basics while building confidence, focus, and emotional control. Classes are structured but playful, blending tradition with modern fun.

What parents love

Family class, ages 7 to adult

Mondays at 6:30 PM. A welcoming all-level class where adults, teens, and kids can train together or come independently. It’s not exclusively for families. It’s simply our “everyone trains together” class. All are welcome:

  • Kids attending alone
  • Adults attending alone
  • Parent and child pairs
  • Beginners and intermediate students

The feeling is lighthearted, energetic, and community-focused.

Advanced karate and kobudō, by invitation

For students ready to study more deeply. This class includes advanced kata, partner work, body mechanics, and traditional Okinawan weapons such as bō and sai. Training emphasizes safety, etiquette, and respect for the classical culture behind the weapons.

8 reasons families choose Roots Dojo

  1. Children learn respect and proper etiquette

    We teach strong, confident responses and greetings in Japanese, along with proper bows and traditional dojo manners. Students also learn seated bowing and meditation at the start and end of each class, just as practiced in authentic martial arts training.

  2. Gentle when needed, firm when needed

    Students learn the difference between good choices and bad ones. When unsafe behavior appears, such as actions that could lead to bullying or accidents, we explain why it’s unacceptable and correct it firmly but fairly.

  3. Growing real confidence

    Instructors immediately praise good effort, attitude, and improvement. This helps children become confident and more willing to try new things. Even if a skill feels difficult at first, consistent practice builds confidence, and advancing in rank or succeeding in matches gives students a powerful sense of accomplishment.

  4. Improved strength and fitness

    Kids build core strength through fun group workouts like sit-ups and push-ups, done in a game-like format so it never feels like a chore. Karate is a full-body activity that improves flexibility, balance, and coordination.

  5. Developing independence, cooperation, and leadership

    Beyond technique, students grow socially through teamwork and shared practice. Each child gets opportunities to lead warm-ups, guide a group, or serve as a team captain, helping them build independence, cooperation, and leadership. Middle- and high-school students with brown belt or higher may also assist with instruction.

  6. Learning Japanese language and culture

    We teach greetings, responses, and etiquette used in Japanese dojos. Every class includes “Mat Chat,” where students learn Japanese proverbs and cultural lessons. Children experience training that feels authentically Japanese, while staying safe and having fun.

  7. Parents and children can train together

    Our dojo offers a special Family Class not found in many other schools. It’s perfect for parents who want to train with their children and build healthy habits together.

  8. Instruction from a teacher who speaks Japanese

    Our director and head instructor, Tobey Stansbury, teaches gently and clearly in Japanese. Even students who are shy with English can relax and enjoy learning alongside their classmates.

Introducing our head instructor

Tobey first began karate at age seven after being inspired by The Karate Kid. Only a few months into training, something remarkable happened: his lifelong stutter disappeared. Through this experience he learned firsthand that karate is a martial art that focuses the whole mind, body, and spirit. He trained under one of the Northwest’s pioneering martial arts masters, Hanshi Morris Mack, and at age twenty-four he traveled to Japan on his own to search for the roots of his karate style.

For three years he lived and trained in Tochigi, Ibaraki, and Tokyo, studying under senior instructors of the same lineage. During this time he also taught English at elementary and junior high schools, strengthening both his Japanese language skills and his cultural understanding.

At age twenty-seven, he returned to the United States and founded Emerald City Karate, a dojo that grew into a beloved community hub in Seattle.

Later, at age forty-four, Tobey founded Roots Dojo in Japan, carrying forward the spirit of joyful training, bilingual instruction, and deep respect for Okinawan karate that shaped his lifelong journey.
