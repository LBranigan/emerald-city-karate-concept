What your first day looks like

A dojo has a few customs. None of them are hard, and we’ll walk you through every one.

Before you come

  • Reserve your classes. Class sizes are capped so everyone gets attention. You’ll pick your first class when you sign up and can book the rest of your two weeks at the same time.
  • Dress to move. Sweats and a T-shirt are perfect. You’ll change into your dojo T-shirt when you arrive.
  • Learn to count to ten. It’s the first Japanese you’ll use in class. Practice it below.

When you arrive

  • Come 5 to 10 minutes early. The front desk will give you your T-shirt and white belt and introduce you to your instructor.
  • Shoes off before the mat. The black mat along the back of the room is fine to walk on in shoes, for trips to the restroom.
  • Bow on and off the mat. A bow toward the front of the room is how everyone here shows respect, as is the custom in Japan. Think of it as a handshake.
  • Keep it quiet during class. Parents are welcome to watch. Calls and conversations wait until afterwards, and everyone stands silently for the opening and closing ceremony.

Count to ten before you walk in

Every class counts its warm-ups, stretches and basics in Japanese. Knowing one to ten is also how a white belt earns the Memory stripe, the first of four on the way to a new belt.

Meet Tobey Sensei

Tobey Stansbury started karate in Yakima at seven years old, with a severe stutter. Within months of training it was gone.

He has been on the mat ever since. He grew up training under Hanshi Morris Mack at the American headquarters of Shudokan karate, then spent two and a half years in Japan studying with the masters of the style. He holds the rank of Go-Dan Shihan, a fifth-degree black belt, and has run Emerald City Karate in Seattle since 2008.

He teaches the way he was taught: the aim of karate is not victory or defeat but the perfection of character.

See all questions and answers

Can we start at any time?

Yes. All three starter classes take new students every week of the year. For ages 7 and up, belt tests are held at the end of January, May and September, so joining at least two months before one gives you time to be ready for it.

How many classes can we come to during the trial?

As many as you like; the trial is unlimited. We suggest once a week for ages 4 to 6 and twice a week from age 7.

Do we need to reserve every class?

Yes. Each class has a set number of places so that it stays safe and everyone gets attention. You’ll reserve your first class when you sign up, and you can book the rest of your two weeks at the same time. If you forget we’ll do our best to fit you in, but when a class is full we may ask you to come to a different one.

Can the trial start on a later date?

Yes. Choose any first class in the next three weeks when you sign up, and your two weeks start that day. If you need a date further out, call or email and we’ll set it for you.

What should we wear?

Loose, comfortable clothes you can move in, like sweats and a T-shirt. Shoes and socks come off for class. You’ll get your dojo T-shirt at the front desk on day one and wear it as your uniform for the trial. A full karate uniform is for sale at the front desk whenever you’d like one.

My child is shy. Can I join in?

Yes. In Little Dragons, parents are welcome on the mat for the whole class or just to hold pads and cheer, at no charge. From age 7, parents can train in the same class as their kids.

Will karate make my child aggressive?

Karate students tend to be more controlled, not less. We teach that martial arts are for defending yourself only in the most extreme situations, and we practice conflict resolution and self-control so that students keep their cool under stress.

Is it safe?

Injuries are very uncommon here. A qualified black belt instructor teaches and supervises every class with safety in mind, and protective gear is required for sparring. Karate is a physical activity, though, and students accept the risks that come with taking part.

We’ve trained at another dojo. Where do we start?

Email us and we’ll arrange a private meetup and assessment to find the right class for you.

How do we keep going after the trial?

Stop by the front desk and we’ll set up a monthly membership with you in person. Nothing is charged automatically when your trial ends.
